Contextualizing Period 1

Learning Objective: Explain the context for European encounters in the Americas from 1491 to 1607.

The history of the Americas prior to and following European contact involves complex interactions among diverse populations. Indigenous societies established vibrant and distinct cultures over thousands of years across North and South America, adapting to vastly different ecosystems and topographies.

Indigenous Societies and European Exploration

Before European arrival, Native American groups formed complex networks based on agriculture, hunting, and trade. The arrival of European explorers in the late 15th century initiated structural shifts in global demographics, commerce, and political dominance.

Transatlantic Connections

The establishment of transatlantic routes facilitated the Columbian Exchange—the widespread transfer of plants, animals, culture, human populations, technology, diseases, and ideas between the Americas, West Africa, and the Old World.
